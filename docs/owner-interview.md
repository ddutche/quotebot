# Owner interview (30 minutes)

Use this for the sit-down with a business owner. Two goals: learn how they price jobs, and find out if they'd pay for this. Take notes in plain words. `rules-author` turns the notes into a rules file afterward.

**Before the call:** run `brand-extractor` on their website so you already know their services and service area.

## Part 1: How they get and answer leads (5 min)

1. Where do most of your leads come from? (Google, word of mouth, Angi/Thumbtack, Facebook, other)
2. When someone asks for a price, what happens next? Walk me through it.
3. How long does it usually take to get a price back to them?
4. What share of people who ask for a price end up booking? Your best guess is fine.
5. What software do you use to run jobs and quotes? (Jobber, Housecall Pro, spreadsheets, paper)

## Part 2: How they price (15 min)

6. What jobs do you do most? Which ones could you price over the phone without seeing them?
7. For each of those: what do you need to know to give a price? (size, count, location, materials, access)
8. What's your price for each? Per hole, per square foot, per hour, flat rate?
9. What makes a job cost more? (ceilings, texture, height, travel, rush)
10. Do you have a trip fee or a minimum job price?
11. What makes you say "I need to see it first"? (these become escalation rules)
12. Any jobs you don't want at all?
13. What ZIP codes or towns do you cover?
14. How often do your prices change?
15. If you gave an online estimate, how wide a range would you be comfortable with? (like 10% under to 20% over)
16. Can you show me 3 recent quotes and what the final invoice was?

## Part 3: Would they pay (10 min)

17. If a bot on your site gave customers a price range any time of day and sent you their name and number, would that help? Why or why not?
18. What would worry you about it?
19. What do you pay for your website and software now each month?
20. Have you tried any quote or chatbot tools? What happened?
21. Would you try a free 30-day pilot if we set everything up?
22. After the pilot, what would it need to do for you to pay for it?

## After the call

- Send a thank-you text the same day.
- Write down the exact words they used about their problem. These go into sales copy later.
- Mark them: **Pilot yes / Maybe / No**, and why.
